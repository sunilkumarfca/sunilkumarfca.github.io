Governance maturity: measuring governance with evidence.

A governance checklist asks whether a policy exists. A governance maturity assessment asks whether it is current, applied consistently, owned, evidenced and embedded in decisions — and places the organisation on a scale from vulnerable to optimised.

This section explains governance maturity for NGOs and mission-driven organisations, how evidence-based assessment works, and what assessment findings reveal. It is the thinking behind the IGMA™ (Institutional Governance Maturity Assessment) method.

Curated by CA Sunil Kumar, FCA — Chartered Accountant and social-sector CFO with 30+ years in NGO finance, audit and governance.

Key concepts

The ideas behind evidence-based governance assessment, and how they connect.

Governance maturity assessment
An assessment of how well governance practices actually work — whether they are current, applied consistently, owned, evidenced and embedded in decisions — rather than simply whether a policy exists. Results are placed on a maturity scale, so an organisation can see where it stands and what to strengthen first. See: Governance Checklist vs Governance Maturity Assessment.
The IGMA™ Governance Maturity Scale
Five levels: L1 Vulnerable, L2 Reactive, L3 Defined, L4 Managed and L5 Optimised. Each question starts at 1.0 and moves up only as the criteria for higher levels are met; anything below 4.0 is treated as meaningful exposure rather than "room to improve". How maturity is scored.
Evidence-based criteria
A criterion counts as met only when it is clearly and consistently true today, with evidence. Partly true or uncertain counts as not met. Approved policies, Board minutes, registers and audit reports are strong evidence; verbal confirmation alone is weak.
Governance Perception Gap™
The difference between perceived maturity — what leadership believes — and demonstrated maturity — what the evidence supports. A leadership team may rate a practice at Level 4 because a process exists, while the evidence shows it is not applied consistently. The gap is a finding in itself: it shows where confidence has run ahead of practice.
IGMA™ (Institutional Governance Maturity Assessment)
The method created by CA Sunil Kumar, FCA, that brings these ideas together: 60 questions across 8 governance pillars, 646 evidence-based criteria, and Board-ready outputs. See the IGMA™ toolkits.
