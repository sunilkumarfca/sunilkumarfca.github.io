Compliant on paper, exposed in practice

Most FCRA trouble does not start with a missing form. It starts with governance: an Audit Committee that never sees the designated account, a conflict of interest between a trustee and a vendor paid from foreign funds, an FCRA risk that is known to staff but never reaches the Board. When the 2026 Rules raise the bar on purposes, key functionaries and disclosure, those gaps are what an inspection or a funder's due diligence finds.

This toolkit asks whether your Board and management actually govern foreign contribution, with the oversight, controls and evidence to show it. Read what three FCRA governance assessments showed for the pattern in practice.
