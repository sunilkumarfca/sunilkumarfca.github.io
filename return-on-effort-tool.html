Free practice tool for individuals · From the book Optimise Your Return on Effort

Where should your effort go?

Ideas are of little value unless we can use them in practical life. This free tool turns the book's SROE (Strategic Return on Effort) framework into practical steps. Start with a 2-minute quick check that gives you a result straight away. When you want the full picture, the deep dive takes about 30 minutes the first time and 15 minutes for a later review.

Use it for a life or career decision, for your spare time, or for spending and saving choices. Deciding for an NGO or organisation? Use the organisation version.

Return on Effort = Value Created ÷ Total Effort Invested. Your answers stay in this browser on your device. Nothing is sent anywhere.

Quick check: is it worth the effort?

Think of a choice you are facing and two options. If waiting or doing nothing is a real option, use it as one of them. Tap one answer for each question.

My result

What a 2-minute check cannot see. It does not weigh each kind of return separately (money, health, family, learning), the hidden costs, what you would give up, how sure you are, or whether you can undo it. The deep dive does, and it also looks at where your week actually goes.

How it works: value = good it does × 0.5 + fit × 0.3 + 3 × 0.2 (future choices taken as neutral); effort and risk = what it takes × 0.6 + downside × 0.4. High = ratio 1.3 or more with at least a partial fit; Low = below 0.9, or a downside you could not cope with.

Step 2: SROE decision test

Does the complete exchange make sense? Not whether an option looks attractive, but what it creates, what it costs and what it displaces. Always include "do nothing" or "delay" as one option.

Allocation check: is my time going where the value is?

Enter what you put into each option today. The tool compares your share of time with each option's share of value.

Give it more = its share of value is at least 10 points higher than its share of your time. Give it less = 10 points lower, or its SROE profile is Low. These are prompts to think, not rules.

Scoring settings (you may change)

Value = Return × + Strategic fit × + Optionality ×

Cost = Effort × + Risk ×

High profile if ratio at least (and fit 3+); Low if below .

Return score = average of the option's three biggest returns. Risk score = average of downside and uncertainty, plus 0.5 if reversible only with some cost, or 1 if not reversible (maximum 5). Any option whose downside you could not survive is marked Low.

This is a decision aid, not a mathematical answer. Use it to make the full exchange visible, then use your own judgement. A good decision can still have a bad outcome.

Step 3: Where my week goes

Your calendar is like an investment statement. List the main things you do in a normal week, such as work, family, exercise, commuting and screen time. For each, give the hours, how useful it is and how it leaves you. That is enough for a suggestion. Add more detail only if you want sharper suggestions.

C. The 10-Year Adequacy Test

"If I continue exactly as I am today for the next ten years, will this still be adequate?" It is a trajectory test, not a forecast.

Likely in 10 years = today's score, plus 1.5 if improving or minus 1.5 if declining (between 1 and 5). Adequate 3.5+ → Protect; At risk 2.5–3.4 → Strengthen; Inadequate below 2.5 → Change.
